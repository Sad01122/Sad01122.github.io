The 10-Second Test — What Is a Clause?

Clause = a group of words that has BOTH a subject AND a verb.

WordsSubject?Verb?What is it?
Jim studiedJimstudied✅ clause
in the Sweet Shop✗ (no doer)✗✅ phrase, not a clause
because Jim studiedJimstudied✅ clause (but dependent!)
🧠
Trick: ask "WHO does the action?" (subject) and "WHAT is happening?" (verb). Both present → clause. Both words in your answer must be in the same group.

Independent vs Dependent Clause

Independent clause = it can live alone

A group of words with subject + verb that expresses a complete thought — it is a sentence.

Jim studied in the Sweet Shop for his chemistry quiz. ✅ complete — standalone.

💡
"So what?" test: read the group of words alone. If your mind asks "…and then? What happened?" it is NOT complete → not independent.

Dependent clause = it needs a partner

Has subject + verb but does NOT express a complete thought — it cannot be a sentence. Usually starts with a dependent marker word.

When Jim studied in the Sweet Shop for his chemistry quiz… 😕 So what? → dependent. Alone it dangles → that's a fragment error.

Dependent Marker Words — Grouped by Meaning

GroupMarkersExample
⏰ TIMEafter, before, when, whenever, while, since, until, onceAfter I submitted the assignment, I slept.
❓ REASONbecause, as, sinceBecause it was raining, we stayed inside.
⚖️ CONDITIONif, unless, whether, even ifIf you revise daily, the quiz will feel easy.
🔄 CONTRASTalthough, though, even though, whileAlthough he studied all night, he failed.
📍 PLACE/OTHERwhere, wherever, that, which, whoThe book that I borrowed is late.
🧠
Shortcut: if the sentence starts with one of these markers, it's almost certainly a dependent clause → you MUST attach it to a complete sentence. Grouping by meaning beats memorizing one giant list.

Joining Two Independent Clauses — Only 5 Legal Ways

#MethodExample
1Period (two sentences)I like this class. It is very interesting.
2Semicolon ; (close ideas)I like this class; it is very interesting.
3Comma + FANBOYSI like this class, and it is very interesting.
4Make one clause dependentI like this class because it is very interesting.
5Combine the ideasThis interesting class is one I like.
🔑
FANBOYS = the 7 coordinating conjunctions: For · And · Nor · But · Or · Yet · So. Comma goes before the FANBOYS word when it joins two complete sentences: Jim studied for his quiz, but it was hard to concentrate.

Independent marker words (start a sentence, join with ;): also, however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, consequently, finally, instead, then. Structure: ; + however, + sentence.

The Three Classic Errors (+ Every Fix)

⚠️
❌ Comma splice — a comma alone joining two complete sentences.
Wrong: I like this class, it is very interesting.
✅ Five fixes: 1) I like this class. It is very interesting. · 2) I like this class; it is very interesting. · 3) I like this class, and it is very interesting. · 4) I like this class because it is very interesting. · 5) Because it is very interesting, I like this class.
⚠️
❌ Fused sentence / run-on — two sentences, NO punctuation.
Wrong: My professor is intelligent I've learned a lot from her.
✅ …intelligent. I've learned… · …intelligent; I've learned… · …intelligent, and I've learned… · …intelligent; moreover, I've learned…
⚠️
❌ Fragment — a dependent clause pretending to be a sentence.
Wrong: Because I forgot the exam was today.
✅ Because I forgot the exam was today, I didn't study. · I forgot the exam was today.
✅
Detection trick: two or more subject–verb pairs in one sentence with no punctuation → run-on. Sentence starts with because/while/which/who/until… → check it's attached → else fragment.

Comma Golden Rule (memorize this one line)

💡
Dependent clause FIRST → comma. Independent clause FIRST → NO comma.
✅ After I finished, I watched a movie. · ✅ I watched a movie after I finished.
Same for FANBOYS: complete-sentence, and complete-sentence.

Phrase❌ never both❌ neverin the morning · the tall man in a blue jacket

She eats eggs in the morning. → clause She eats eggs + phrase in the morning.
Fast test: point at a group of words — "WHO does the action?" If no subject doing a verb → phrase. Meows so loudly 😕 (who?) → phrase. That cat meows so loudly ✅ → clause.

The HEAD Trick — Every Phrase Has One Boss Word

the tall man in a blue jacket ↓ ↓ ↑ ↓ det adj HEAD postmodifier
💡
Cover everything except the head — what's left is the phrase's type & job.
the tall man in a blue jacket → cover modifiers → man (a noun) → noun phrase.
aware of the danger → aware (an adjective) → adjective phrase.
very well → well (an adverb) → adverb phrase.

The Four Phrase Types

TypeFormulaExampleIts job?
🧢 Noun Phrase (NP)(Det) + (Adj) + HEAD NOUN + (Post)the tall man in a blue jacket · a feeling of isolationSubject / object / object of preposition
📐 Prepositional (PP)Preposition + noun phraseunder the table (WHERE) · after noon (WHEN) · on time (HOW)Usually adverb job (answers where/when/how)

🔑
Job tests: replace the phrase with he/she/it → still works = noun's job. Ask "which one? / what kind?" → answer = adjective's job (Cats from the alleys… → WHICH cats?). Ask when/where/how/why → answer = adverb's job (She eats bread in the morning = WHEN).

The Trap: Adjective Phrase vs Adjective Clause

Adj phraseAdj clause
subject + verb?❌✅
exampleafraid of dogswho didn't pass inspection

✅
Rule: a clause has a mini-sentence inside it (subject+verb); a phrase doesn't. That's the whole test.

Common Mistakes (❌ wrong / ✅ right)

  • ❌ Standalone phrase = fragment — In the morning. (as a short answer to "when?" it's fine; as a sentence, no.) ✅ I run in the morning.
  • ❌ Double head — The box is on the table is broken. ✅ The box on the table is broken.
  • ❌ Fake subject in a phrase — he in the morning. ✅ clause needs a real verb: he runs in the morning.
  • ✅ Ending with a preposition (Put it on.) is fine in modern English — but The table is on is incomplete → ✅ The table is on the ground.

🧠 The Big Trick — It's Not 12 Rules, It's a 3 × 4 Grid

SIMPLE
(a fact)		CONTINUOUS
(in progress)		PERFECT
(already done)		PERF. CONTINUOUS
(how long)
PRESENTI workI am workingI have workedI have been working
PASTI workedI was workingI had workedI had been working
FUTUREI will workI will be workingI will have workedI will have been working
🧠
Three tricks inside the grid:
1) The name builds the formula: past perfect continuous = past (had) + perfect (been) + continuous (-ing) → had been working.
2) Aspects never change meaning: simple = fact/habit · continuous = be+ing (in progress) · perfect = have+V3 (done; links times) · PC = have been+ing (duration).
3) Time only changes the helping verb: present = (nothing)/have · past = had/was-were · future = will.
💡
Picture trick — draw a timeline: PAST —— 📍NOW ——→ FUTURE. Continuous = a wavy line (action stretched out) · Perfect = an arrow pointing back (finished before another time) · Perfect continuous = wavy line + arrow (duration up to a point).

🔑
"Can you name when?" test: specific finished time → past simple (I lived there in 2019); unspecified / still true now → present perfect (I have lived here for 5 years). for = length of time · since = starting point (since 2023).

🧩 Building Tricks — Questions, Negatives, -s

  • First helping verb moves in questions/negatives: Has she been studying? · She hasn't been studying.
  • Only simple present & past need do/does/did — every other tense already has an auxiliary: Does she study? · Is she studying? · Will she study?
  • Third-person -s: he/she/it → works, goes, teaches (#1 spoken-English mistake worldwide).
  • After did, use V1: Did you go ✅ — Did you went ❌.
  • Stative verbs (know, understand, love, want, need, seem) → never continuous: I understand ✅ — I am understanding ❌.

📚 Learn These First + Practice That Works

✅
Research-backed order: 1) Present Simple → 2) Present Continuous → 3) Past Simple → 4) will / going to → 5) Present Perfect — these five cover ~95% of everyday English. Then: Past Continuous → Past Perfect → Present Perfect Continuous → future continuous/perfect last (rare, even for natives).
  • One-verb drill: take study and fill every grid cell: I study · I am studying · I studied · I have studied… then repeat with verbs from YOUR life (work, play, cook).
  • Rewrite a paragraph in the past, then in the future (forces tense switching).
  • Cover the examples in the tables above and make your own sentence per tense — passive reading → active grammar.

🧊 The Iceberg Model — What Culture Really Is

Culture = shared values, norms and assumptions that guide behaviour (Hofstede's words: "collective programming of the mind"). Think of an iceberg: you see only the tip — the huge part that actually controls behaviour is underwater.

🌊 ABOVE water (visible, ~10%)🧊 BELOW water (hidden, ~90%)
Dress · language · food · greetings · music · festivals · gestures · office layout Values · beliefs · assumptions · attitude to authority & time · fairness, family, respect · world view
🔑
Golden line (memorize): most workplace conflict comes from the hidden layer, not the visible one. Also: culture is a tendency, not a stereotype — individuals vary. The10%/90% figures are a teaching metaphor, not measured data.

🧅 Hofstede's Onion — Peel the Layers Outside → In

LayerWhat it isExample
🔤 SymbolsWords, gestures, dress, objects only insiders understandBlack clothes at a funeral · school uniform
🦸 HeroesReal or imaginary people who are models for behaviourThe cricketer everyone copies
🎭 RitualsCollective activities that seem unnecessary but bind the groupBirthday song · Eid embrace · assembly
❤️ ValuesBroad preferences learned early, mostly unconscious — the CORE"Respect elders" · "honesty first"
🧠
Symbols + Heroes + Rituals = "Practices" → visible to outsiders and they change fast. Values = the core → invisible and changes slowly. Exam line: "practices change faster than values."

⚖️ Iceberg vs Onion — Who Made What? (common mix-up!)

⚠️
The mix-up: the iceberg belongs to HALL, not Hofstede. Hofstede's own hidden-core picture is the onion. Both say the same thing: understand the values beneath the behaviour.

🧱 What Is a Paragraph? — The 2 Laws

A paragraph = one group of sentences that develops ONE main idea.

LawMeaningTest
Unityone idea per paragraphcan every sentence be linked back to the topic sentence?
Coherencelogical order + transitionsread it aloud — does each sentence pull the next forward?

Length: 3–7 sentences (exam paragraph ≈ 80–120 words) · order: general → specific → wrap-up · new idea = new paragraph.

🧠
The one-sentence test for unity: cover the paragraph, read only the topic sentence, then ask of every other sentence "does this prove or explain it?" No → cut it, or start a new paragraph.

🦴 Anatomy of a Paragraph — 3 Parts

PartJobPosition
① Topic sentencenames the topic and what you say about it — the most general sentenceusually first
② Supporting sentencesprove the topic sentence: facts, examples, reasons, quotesmiddle (2–4 sentences)
③ Concluding sentenceechoes the main idea in fresh words, shows how the details fitlast
✅
Fast map: general (topic) → specific (details) → general again (conclusion). A paragraph that starts specific and ends on a brand-new fact has no shape — and no marks.

🎯 The Topic Sentence — Too Broad / Too Narrow / Just Right

❌ Too broad❌ Too narrow✅ Just right
"Libraries are interesting.""The library opens at 9 a.m.""A public library offers far more than books to borrow."
nothing arguable — no directiona single fact — nothing left to developtopic (library) + claim (more than books)

A good topic sentence: ① names the topic AND what you say about it · ② is the most general sentence (no detailed support) · ③ usually comes first · ④ is neither too broad nor too narrow.

🧠
Write it as Topic + Comment. Topic = a public library · Comment = offers far more than books to borrow. If you can't split your sentence into those two halves, it isn't a topic sentence yet.

🔧 Supporting Details — the 4 Kinds + the Cut Test

KindLooks likeExample (library paragraph)
Fact / statistica number or verifiable statement"A library card gives free access to thousands of titles."
Examplea specific case"For instance, a student can book a quiet study room."
Explanation / reasonwhy the claim is true"This helps because many homes are too noisy to study in."
Quote / expert viewsomeone else's wordsA librarian explains, "Our doors are open to everyone."
✂️
The cut test: "would this sentence still make sense if I removed it?" If it does not support the topic sentence → cut it. Three details is the exam-safe number — and they must be different from each other.

🏁 The Concluding Sentence — Wrap Up, Don't Repeat

✅ A good conclusion…❌ What fails
echoes the main idea in fresh words"Libraries offer more than books." (copy-paste)
shows how the details fit together"That is all about libraries." (abrupt, no idea)
adds no brand-new evidencea new fact that should have been a body detail
may lead into the next paragraphan idea left hanging with no link

Strong example: "In short, the library is a shared learning space that serves the whole community, not just readers."

Openers: In short, · Therefore, · Overall, · In conclusion, · To sum up,

Support 1First, it provides quiet, free study spaces where students can concentrate without paying for a café table.
Support 2Second, many libraries give community members free computers, internet and printing, which helps people who lack these at home.
Support 3Finally, libraries host workshops, reading clubs and story hours that bring neighbours together.
CONCLUSIONIn short, the library is a shared learning space that serves the whole community, not just readers.

Why it scores full marks: one idea (more than books) · three different kinds of support (space · technology · events) · sequence transitions (First / Second / Finally) · conclusion in fresh words.

① Why volunteering matters

Volunteering matters because it strengthens both the community that receives help and the volunteer who gives it. To begin with, even a few hours a week can fill gaps that paid staff cannot cover, such as tutoring children or visiting elderly patients. In addition, volunteers learn practical skills — communication, teamwork and time management — that employers value in every field. Finally, working beside people from different backgrounds builds empathy and confidence, which no textbook can teach. In short, volunteering is not simply free labour; it is a two-way exchange that leaves both the helper and the helped stronger.

PartTextWhy it works
Topic"…strengthens both the community… and the volunteer…"a two-sided claim → two halves to develop
Detail 1To begin with, tutoring children or visiting elderly patientsconcrete examples of the gap being filled
Detail 2In addition, communication, teamwork, time managementa reason aimed at the volunteer
Detail 3Finally, empathy and confidencea deeper benefit, not a repeat of detail 2
ConclusionIn short, … a two-way exchangeechoes "both sides" in fresh words

≈ 100 words · transitions: To begin with · In addition · Finally · In short.

② Benefits of learning an instrument

Learning a musical instrument benefits students long after the last note is played. First, playing an instrument trains concentration, because reading notes and coordinating both hands demand full attention for long periods. In addition, music practice is linked to better memory and stronger problem-solving skills, since musicians must recognise patterns and correct mistakes instantly. Moreover, learning an instrument teaches patience and discipline, because progress comes only through regular, deliberate practice. Therefore, even a beginner who never performs in public gains sharper thinking and steadier self-control.

PartTextWhy it works
Topic"…benefits students long after the last note is played."a claim with a time angle, not "music is good"
Detail 1First, … trains concentration …benefit 1 + a because-reason
Detail 2In addition, … memory and problem-solving …benefit 2 + a since-reason
Detail 3Moreover, … patience and discipline …a character benefit, so it doesn't overlap
ConclusionTherefore, … sharper thinking and steadier self-control.ties all three benefits into one line

≈ 90 words · transitions: First · In addition · Moreover · Therefore.

③ A place you love

A place I love is my grandfather's rooftop in Lahore, where the city feels far away and close at the same time. To begin with, the roof is cool and quiet in the evening, so it is the only spot where I can read without interruption. In addition, from the parapet I can watch kites circling above the old houses and hear the call to prayer drifting from three mosques at once. Finally, my cousins and I spread a mat there after dinner, drink tea and talk until the stars appear. In short, that small rooftop is not a grand place, but it is where my family and my memories meet.

PartTextWhy it works
Topic"…my grandfather's rooftop in Lahore, where…"specific topic + a comment on why it matters
Detail 1To begin with, cool and quiet in the eveningsense of sound/quiet + personal use
Detail 2In addition, kites … call to prayersight + sound — concrete images
Detail 3Finally, cousins, mat, tea, starspeople + memory (the emotional detail)

📝 Likely Exam Questions

  1. "Write a paragraph of 80–120 words on [topic]. It must have a clear topic sentence, three supporting details and a concluding sentence." → use the 5-line frame above.
  2. "Identify the topic sentence, supporting details and concluding sentence in the paragraph below." → label TOPIC / SUPPORT 1–3 / CONCLUSION, like the annotated sample.
  3. "Which sentence does not belong in this paragraph, and why?" → it breaks unity (it is about a different topic).
  4. "Differentiate between a thesis statement and a topic sentence." → whole essay vs one paragraph; umbrella vs the person under it.
  5. "Rewrite this paragraph for better coherence." → add transitions, cut the off-topic sentence, paraphrase the conclusion.

🔄 Self-Quiz (tap to flip)

Q1

What are the three parts of a paragraph?

↻ tap
A

Topic sentence → supporting details → concluding sentence (general → specific → general).

Q2

Fix this topic sentence: "Sports are good."

↻ tap
A

Too broad. Add a comment: "Team sports teach students cooperation and discipline."

Q3

Thesis or topic sentence? — "Public libraries strengthen communities in three ways."

↻ tap
A

Thesis — it controls the whole essay and promises three body paragraphs.

Q4

A paragraph about reading contains a sentence about football. Which rule is broken?

↻ tap
A

Unity — one idea per paragraph. Cut the sentence or start a new paragraph.

Q5

Name two transitions for adding and one for concluding.

↻ tap
A

Adding: in addition, moreover (also, furthermore). Concluding: in short (overall, therefore).

Q6

What may a concluding sentence NEVER add?

↻ tap
A

Brand-new evidence / a new fact. It echoes the main idea in fresh words and shows how the details fit.

Remember (3 bullets): one idea per paragraph · topic sentence = topic + comment, and every detail must support it · transitions + a paraphrased conclusion are the cheapest marks in the paper.
