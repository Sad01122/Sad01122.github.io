🧱 Clauses — Independent vs Dependent

IndependentDependent
Hassubject + verb + complete thoughtsubject + verb + a marker word
Can stand alone?Yes — it's a sentenceNo — needs an independent partner
Examplethe sun was shiningbecause the sun was shining

Marker words by meaning: TIME (after, before, when, while, since, until) · REASON (because, as, since) · CONDITION (if, unless, whether) · CONTRAST (although, though, even though).

🧠
FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So → join two independent clauses with comma + FANBOYS.
Transition words (however, therefore, moreover) join with ; + word,.
5 fixes for a comma splice / run-on / fragment: period · semicolon · comma+FANBOYS · make one clause dependent · combine into one sentence.

🏷️ Phrases — the 4 Types (no subject + verb inside!)

TypeHead word is a…Answers…Example
Noun phrasenounwho/whatthe tall boy · a glass of water
Prepositional phraseprepositionwhere/when/howunder the table · in the morning
Adjective phraseadjectivedescribes a nounvery tall · afraid of the dark
Adverb phraseadverbdescribes verb/adj/advvery quickly · in a hurry
✅
The2-step test:① Can you find a subject and a verb together? → then it's a clause.
② Find the head word (the most important word) → that head decides the phrase type. ⚠️ "under the table" is a prepositional phrase even though it tells where (its job is adverb-like, but its head is a preposition).

🧠
The name IS the formula: past + perfect + continuous = had + been + -ing. Time changes only the helping verb; the aspect never changes.

Signal words: every day/usually → pres. simple · now/look/at this moment → pres. cont. · yesterday/last year/ago → past simple · since/for/already/yet → present perfect · by the time/before/after (two pasts) → past perfect · tomorrow/next week → future.

🧊🧅 Cross-Culture — Iceberg, Onion & the6 Dimensions

Hall's Iceberg (1976)Hofstede's Onion (1980/2010)
Above water (visible): dress, food, language, greetings, gesturesOutside layers = Practices: Symbols → Heroes → Rituals (change fast)
Below water (hidden): values, beliefs, attitude to authority/time/familyCore = Values (invisible, learned early, change slowly)
Key line: most conflict comes from the hidden layerKey line: practices change faster than values
🔑
The famous mix-up: the Iceberg belongs to HALL, not Hofstede — Hofstede's hidden-core picture is the onion. Both say: understand the values beneath the behaviour. The10%/90% split is a metaphor, not data.
DimensionKid question🇵🇰 Pakistan🇺🇸 USA
Individualism vs Collectivism"I win" or "we win"?14 (collectivist)91 (individualist)
Uncertainty Avoidance"Tell me EXACTLY the rules!"70 (high)46
Power DistanceCan juniors question the boss?5540
Also: Masculinity/Femininity · Long-/Short-term Orientation · Indulgence/RestraintJapan = highest masculinity + uncertainty avoidance

Hall's context & time: high-context (PK, Japan — meaning in tone/silence/relationships, trust-based) vs low-context (USA, Germany — meaning in explicit words, contracts) · polychronic (relationships beat schedules) vs monochronic (punctuality).

7 PK-vs-global conflicts: ① hierarchy & silence · ② sifarish vs merit · ③ polite "no" (InshaAllah, dekhte hain) · ④ time flexibility · ⑤ gender norms (Harassment Act2010) · ⑥ faith/Ramadan · ⑦ remote teams. CQ (Earley & Ang) = Drive · Knowledge · Strategy · Action.

✅
Conflict = misread signals, not bad intent → pause → hypothesise → adapt → confirm. Culture is a tendency, not a stereotype.

📧 Formal Email — Frames, Registers, Phrases

InformalSemi-formalFormal
Readerfriends/close colleaguesknown colleagues/managersseniors, HR, new clients
GreetingHey Sam,Hi Sara,Dear Mr Khan, / Dear Sir or Madam,
Sign-offCheers,Best regards,Yours sincerely, / Kind regards,
WordsI'm, you're, slangCould we…? politeI am, I would (full forms)

6-part anatomy: ① subject line · ② greeting · ③ purpose in the first1–2 lines · ④ body (one idea per paragraph) · ⑤ closing ask with a deadline · ⑥ sign-off + signature.

💡
5-step frame with BLUF = Bottom Line Up Front: Subject (Leave request:19–21 October) → Greeting → Purpose first ("I am writing to request…") → Details + ask + deadline ("Please reply by Friday") → Close ("Kind regards").

You-attitude: ✗ "We cannot refund without a receipt." → ✓ "Return the item with your receipt to receive a full refund."

Openers: "Thank you for your email of14 June." · "I am writing to enquire about…" · "Further to our phone call…" · "As you requested, I am attaching…"
Closers: "I look forward to hearing from you." · "Please feel free to contact me."

⚠️
Never in formal mail: Hi/Hello opener · Cheers/Thanks!/TTYL closing · slang, emojis, ALL CAPS · text-speak (u, asap, thx) · comma splices · forget the attachment. Do: specific subject · proofread · check the recipient field · reply promptly.

6 email kinds: internal · external · sales · routine · personalized · circulars. Letter types: personal · inquiry (intro/body/conclusion) · complaint · sales (headline→intro→body→call to action).

🎭 Mids Mock Test — 15 MCQs

⏱️ ~15 min🎯 aim ≥ 12/154 tenses · 3 clauses · 2 phrases · 2 PoS · 2 email · 2 cross-culture ✅ instant marking + explanations

1She ___ to school every day. (go)Tenses

Answer: B — goes. "Every day" = habit → present simple, and he/she/it takes -s. ❌ go forgets the -s · going needs is · gone is V3.

2By the time we reached the station, the train ___. (leave)Tenses

Answer: C — had left. Two past actions: the train left before we reached → the earlier one takes the past perfect (had + V3); the later one stays past simple.

3I ___ my keys, so I cannot open the door now. (lose)Tenses

Answer: C — have lost. Past action with a result right now (I still can't get in) → present perfect. Pure past story time would be lost.

4Look! The kids ___ in the garden. (play)Tenses

Answer: B — are playing. Signal word "Look!" = happening at this moment → present continuous (am/is/are + -ing).

5"Because it rained heavily, the match was cancelled." — the underlined part is a…Clauses

Answer: B — dependent clause. It has a subject (it) + verb (rained) → so it's a clause; the marker because makes it dependent (cannot stand alone).

6I was very tired, ___ I kept working.Clauses

Answer: B — but. The two halves contrast (tired vs kept working). A comma + FANBOYS joins two independent clauses; because/although would need a full dependent clause after them.

7Which sentence is a comma splice?Clauses

Answer: B. Two independent clauses joined by only a comma = comma splice. ✅ (a) comma+FANBOYS · (c) semicolon · (d) one clause made dependent.

8"The cat hid under the table." That phrase is a…Phrases

Answer: D — prepositional phrase. Head word = under (a preposition), followed by its object. It tells where (an adverb-like job), but the head decides the name.

9"The very tall boy." — the phrase is a…Phrases

Answer: B. Inside the phrase, the head is tall (adjective) and very only intensifies it. Boy is outside the phrase — it's the noun the phrase describes.

10In "She spoke honestly" — honestly is an…Parts of Speech

Answer: A — adverb. It describes the verb "spoke" (how?) and ends in -ly. Adjectives describe nouns/pronouns, not verbs.

11Which of these words is a preposition?Parts of Speech

Answer: A — beneath. It shows place/relationship before a noun ("beneath the tree"). Rapid = adjective · rapidly = adverb (-ly) · rapidity = noun (-ity).

12You do not know the recipient's name. The correct formal greeting is…Email

Answer: B. Formal mail never opens with Hi/Hello/Hey. If you know the name use Dear Mr Smith,; if not, Dear Sir or Madam, (or "To Whom It May Concern").

13In the 5-step formal email frame, BLUF stands for…Email

Answer: B — Bottom Line Up Front. Put your purpose and the action first, details after (Sehgal, HBR2016). Busy readers only read the first lines.

14The Cultural Iceberg model of culture was given by…Cross-Culture

Answer: B — Hall (1976). The classic mix-up is crediting Hofstede — his hidden-core picture is the onion (Symbols → Heroes → Rituals → Values).

15Pakistan scores 14 and the USA 91 on which Hofstede dimension?Cross-Culture

Answer: C — Individualism vs Collectivism. 14 = strongly collectivist (group before self) vs USA 91 = individualist. The other pairs are the distractors' own scores:70/46 = uncertainty avoidance,55/40 = power distance.

Q1 · Convert the non-formal sentences to formal (15 marks — 5 each)

(a) "yo dude, send me that file asap!! thx"
(b) "hey sir, i need leave tmrw n day after, lemme know ok?"
(c) "u didnt reply to my mail lol, guess u dont care."

Model answers

(a) "Dear Mr Ahmed,
Could you please send me the file by today? Thank you for your help.
Kind regards, Ali"

(b) "Dear Sir,
I would like to request leave for tomorrow and the following day (20–21 October). I will complete my pending work before leaving.
Yours sincerely, Hamza"

(c) "Dear Mr Khan,
I wrote to you on12 October and have not yet received a reply. Could you let me know when to expect one?
Best regards, Sana"

What examiners tick (5 each): formal greeting (Dear …) · full forms (I would like, not i need) · no slang/text-speak (yo, dude, lol, tmrw) · polite request wording (Could you please…) · proper sign-off.

Q2 · Convert the paragraph (15 marks)

Convert the following non-formal chat paragraph into a formal, well-structured paragraph (use BLUF: purpose first, then details, then the action + deadline).

💬
"hey team so uh the website is gonna be late again lol. our guy got sick n the tests rnt done n the client keeps ringing me. so dont ask me for it this friday, maybe next week?? lemme know if thats cool, thx!!"

Model answer

Subject: Revised delivery date for the website

"Dear Team,
I am writing to inform you that the website delivery will move from Friday,23 October to Wednesday,28 October. The delay is due to the lead developer's illness and the pending testing, and the client has been notified of the revised date.
I will share a corrected test plan by tomorrow and complete all checks before the new deadline. Please send me any concerns by Thursday so we can resolve them in time.
Kind regards, Hamza Ali"

Why it scores15: formal greeting · BLUF (new date in line1 of the body) · exact dates, no lol/asap/next week maybe · one idea per paragraph · a clear action + deadline · polite close.

Q6 · Convert the clauses into phrases (10 marks — 2 each)

Convert each clause (it has a subject + verb) into a phrase (no subject + verb) without changing the meaning.

(a) the book which is on the table
(b) the man who is at the door
(c) because it was raining, we stayed inside
(d) the students who are in my class
(e) he is tired because he worked all night

#Clause→ Phrase
(a)which is on the tableon the table (prepositional phrase)
(b)who is at the doorat the door (prepositional phrase)

Q7 · Scenario Question — Identify the ICEBERG Model Terms (10 marks)

scenario
A Dutch manager joins a Pakistani company. At his first meeting he asks, "Anyone see a problem with this plan?" — no junior staff member speaks; the senior manager speaks last and everyone simply agrees with him. Staff stand when he enters and call him "Sir". The meeting begins with chai and ten minutes of asking about each other's families. When he asks "Can we finish by Friday?", the team replies "InshaAllah, we will try" — but nothing is delivered. The Dutch manager is angry: "They agreed to Friday!" The Pakistani team is hurt: "He pressured us in front of everyone — have some respect."

(a) Name the model that separates culture into visible and hidden parts, and sort the bold items above into "above the surface" and "below the surface". (4)
(b) Which Hofstede Onion layer do the chai and family questions belong to? What about "respect for elders/authority"? (2)
(c) Which Hofstede dimension and score gap best explains the silence and the title "Sir"? (2)
(d) Which of the 7 PK-vs-global conflicts is the "InshaAllah" episode, and give the CQ-based fix? (2)

Model answer

(a) Hall's Cultural Iceberg (1976) — behaviour we see vs values we can't:

🌊 Above the surface (visible)🧊 Below the surface (hidden)
Standing up · "Sir" · starting with chai · family talk · the words "InshaAllah, we will try" Attitude to authority (juniors don't challenge) · group harmony/consensus · face & respect (no public pressure) · the indirect "no" · relationship-first thinking

(b) Onion: chai rituals and family questions = Rituals (practices — visible, change fast); "respect for elders/authority" = Values (the invisible core, learned early, change slowly). Both sit in the hidden layer of the iceberg.

(c) Power Distance — Pakistan 55 vs USA 40 (and the related Individualism gap, 14 vs91): juniors accept unequal power, disagree privately, and use titles; the Dutch read silence as agreement and behave flatly (low power distance).

(d) Conflict #3 — saying "no" & indirect feedback ("InshaAllah / dekhte hain / try karenge" may be a polite no). Fix (CQ Strategy + Action): pause → check the assumption → confirm commitments in writing, rephrase "try" as yes/no with conditions, ask juniors last or privately — then adapt your own style. Remember: misread signals, not bad intent.

Marking: model named + correct sorting (4) · both onion layers right (2) · dimension + score (2) · conflict #3 + fix (2).
